When you feel completely worn out, let down by people, and deeply alone, the first thing to do is stop carrying the heavy load by yourself and tell God how tired you are. He does not reject your sadness, nor is He surprised by your tears. Relief begins when you let go of that hurt and let God restore your peace from the inside out.

### When You Get Tired of Doing What Is Right

Pull up a chair, take a deep breath, and let us talk like old friends. I know that heavy feeling in your chest. You wake up early, give your best, help others, care for your family, and try to live honestly. Yet, instead of thanks, you get cold looks, gossip, or unfair complaints. It makes you want to drop everything and walk away, doesn't it?

If you feel that way, you are not alone. Long ago, a man named Jeremiah felt the exact same pain. He was not an untouchable statue from an old book; he was a real person just like us. Day after day, he tried to do what was right, but the people around him only laughed at him, criticized him, and left him all alone.

It hurt so much that Jeremiah cried out: “How terrible for me, my mother! Why did you give me birth just to face constant fighting? I have not lent or borrowed money, yet everyone curses me” (Jeremiah 15:10). When our hopes break down, our hearts sink into disappointment. We start thinking that being kind and honest is no longer worth the trouble.

### The Trap of Bitterness

When disappointment is ignored, it slowly turns into a bitter poison called resentment. Jeremiah looked at his life and thought: “Lord, I welcomed Your words with joy. I stayed away from bad crowds and chose what is right. So why am I hurting so much?” (Jeremiah 15:15-17).

Have you ever thought something like that? “After everything I did for them, is this how they treat me?” It is normal to feel that sting, but keeping score only hurts you. Replaying someone’s bad behavior over and over is like drinking poison and hoping the other person gets sick.

The Bible teaches us about God’s grace—His generous love given as a free gift. That love does not work like a soda machine where we insert good deeds and demand an easy life. When we forget this truth, our pain turns into anger, and we feel like the most mistreated person in the world.

### The Deep Pit of Self-Pity

Jeremiah kept sinking until he fell into self-pity, which means feeling terribly sorry for yourself. In verse 18, he hits rock bottom and asks: “Why does my wound never heal? Why are You to me like a dried-up stream, like water that promises relief in the desert but turns out to be fake?”

Notice how honest he was. Jeremiah was so exhausted that he felt God had failed him. Maybe that is how you feel right now: dried up inside, worn out by life, and wondering if your prayers hit the ceiling and bounce right back down.

Self-pity puts a thick blindfold over our eyes. It makes us focus only on our own pain and forget every blessing we have already received. When we lock ourselves in our hurt, we shut the door on everyone who cares, and that is why we end up feeling so lonely.

### God’s Gentle Invitation

Look closely at how God treated Jeremiah. God did not shout at him or walk away. He welcomed him with open arms, but He also offered strong medicine: “If you return to Me, I will restore you. If you separate what is precious from what is worthless, you will speak for Me” (Jeremiah 15:19).

Turning back to God is not about feeling guilty or beating yourself up. It simply means turning around when you are on the wrong road. God was saying like a loving Father: “My child, stop chewing on bitter thoughts that sour your soul. Throw out the trash and keep only what is good.”

Then, God promised to make Jeremiah strong like a wall of bronze against tough times (Jeremiah 15:20-21). God did not take away the difficult people, but He promised to stand right beside him. In life, we must learn this lesson: we need thick skin to handle the world, but a soft heart to keep on loving.

### A Simple Practical Next Step

To break free from this exhaustion today, you do not need to fix your whole life at once. Take one simple, practical step right now:

1. Grab a sheet of paper and draw a line down the middle to make two columns.
2. In the first column, write down your hurts, disappointments, and complaints. When you finish, tear that side off, throw it in the trash, and tell God you are choosing not to carry that heavy weight today.
3. In the second column, write down three simple things you can thank God for right now, like a cool glass of water, fresh air to breathe, or someone who loves you.
4. Say this short prayer: “Lord, I am tired and hurt, but I give my pain to You. Please wash my heart clean, give me fresh peace, and help me take the next step. Amen.”

You are not forgotten. God is right beside you this minute, ready to lift your burden and walk with you every step of the way.
