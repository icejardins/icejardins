We get so angry because the truth crashes directly into our pride and ruins the comfortable illusion that we have everything under control. When someone points out a real flaw or a bad habit in our life, our quick reaction is rarely gratitude. Instead, our walls go up and we strike back to protect our image. This anger comes from a deep fear of being wrong and the painful thought of having to change our ways.

### Trying to Burn the Mirror

Think about an old story from the Bible, in the book of the prophet Jeremiah. God gave a simple man a message of warning for the king of the land, a ruler named Jehoiakim. God spoke with great patience and kindness, saying: "If you will just turn around and stop doing what is wrong, I will forgive everything and fix what is broken in your life." It was a wonderful chance for a fresh start.

Do you know what the king did when a servant read the scroll to him? It was a cold winter day, and the king was sitting comfortably by a warm fireplace. As the messenger read each small part, the king took out a small knife, cut that piece off the scroll, and threw it right into the flames. He kept doing this section by section until the entire message was reduced to ashes. The king truly thought that by destroying the paper, his problem would simply disappear.

We might shake our heads at how silly that was, but we do the exact same thing every day. When a doctor tells us our health is in danger, we complain that the clinic is exaggerating. When our spouse gently mentions that our words were unkind, we turn away and blame our tiring day at work. We try to burn the warning so we do not have to look at our own mistakes in the mirror.

### Throwing the Messenger into the Mud

The Bible story continues with another stubborn leader named Zedekiah. Instead of fixing his mistakes and helping his people, he and his officials decided to silence Jeremiah for good. They grabbed the prophet and lowered him down with ropes into a dark, dry well. There was no clean water at the bottom, only deep, sticky mud. Jeremiah slowly sank down into the muck simply because he refused to tell sweet lies to please powerful people.

Trying to silence those who tell us the truth is an old human habit. It is like covering the red warning light on your car dashboard with a piece of dark tape. The annoying light is hidden, but the engine is still overheating and will soon break down on the highway. When we yell at people who correct us, we throw our relationships into a pit of cold bitterness. We decide that being right is more important than having peace in our homes.

When we reject an uncomfortable truth, we end up stuck in the mud of our own selfish pride. We lose our sleep, we lose our quiet joy, and we push away the few people who truly care enough to help us grow.

### A Helping Hand to Pull Us Out

Right in the middle of that terrible moment, an ordinary man named Ebed-Melech stepped up. He saw the cruel thing done to Jeremiah and went straight to the king to ask for help. He brought soft old rags and ropes so the rough cords would not cut into Jeremiah's arms, and he gently pulled the prophet out of the mud. He put his own safety on the line just to help someone who spoke the truth.

This picture shows us how God cares for us in our daily walk. His deep love—what the Bible calls grace—often reaches us through brave and honest people. A true friend is not someone who claps for our foolish choices. A true friend is someone who reaches out a loving hand and warns us before we fall off a steep cliff.

Hearing the truth always burns like rubbing alcohol poured on a dirty scrape. At first, it stings so sharply that you want to pull away and yell. But that brief sting is what cleans out the wound and keeps infection from spreading. Accepting that someone else is right is the first step toward real healing for our weary hearts.

### The High Cost of Stubborn Pride

We often think to ourselves: "If I admit that I made a mistake, people will look down on me and think I am weak." King Zedekiah believed that exact lie. He feared what other people thought of him much more than he respected what God told him. The result was heartbreaking: he lost his family, his freedom, and his kingdom. He tried so hard to protect his appearance that he ended up losing everything.

Being humble always costs something. It hurts to swallow our pride, say we are sorry, and change our daily habits. But staying stubborn costs so much more. It costs sleepless nights, broken marriages, distant children, and a heavy spirit that ruins our health. God never gives us hard truths to embarrass us or put us down. He speaks them because He loves us too much to let us destroy our lives.

### A Simple Next Step for Today

Think back to a recent conversation that made your temper rise. Maybe your partner pointed out an annoying habit, your boss corrected your work, or your child spoke up about your harsh tone. Instead of making excuses or holding a grudge, take a slow, deep breath. Offer a short prayer: "Lord, help me see my mistake clearly."

Then, go to that person today and say: "I thought about what you told me, and you were right. I am working on it." That simple, honest sentence will melt your anger away and bring true peace into your day.
