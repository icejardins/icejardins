We prefer soothing lies because facing the truth touches our wounds and asks us to make hard changes. A soft lie feels like a quick painkiller, while the real truth asks us to admit our mistakes and face life as it is. Even when the truth stings at first, only honesty can heal our broken hearts and bring us lasting peace.

Pull up a chair and let us talk quietly, like two friends sharing a warm cup of coffee. We all have had days when we wished our troubles would just vanish on their own. But pretending a hole in the roof is not there will never keep the rain out.

### The fear of what others might think

Have you ever ridden on a packed bus when a child blurted out a blunt truth? Picture a quiet bus and a small boy pointing his finger: "Mom, it stinks in here! I want to get off!" His parents turn red and rush to cover his mouth, while the other passengers look away and act like nothing happened.

That child only said what everyone was already smelling, yet saying it out loud caused great embarrassment. We spend much of our time worrying about keeping up appearances. We fear looking foolish, being judged by coworkers, or losing face in front of family and friends.

Long ago, the prophet Jeremiah felt that same heavy burden. God called him to tell the people that they were treating each other badly and turning away from good. Jeremiah went to the temple and spoke with plain honesty. He warned that if they did not change their ways, their city would fall into ruins.

The crowd responded with anger. Instead of looking at their own faults, they wanted to beat Jeremiah and end his life, calling him a traitor. It hurts to hear that we are going the wrong way, and we often attack the person who holds up the mirror.

### The hard medicine that protects our life

Soon after, things grew even harder. A strong foreign army surrounded their land, and God gave Jeremiah a strange task. He told the prophet to put a heavy wooden yoke over his neck, just like the wooden beam used to guide working oxen in the field.

Jeremiah walked down the streets carrying that heavy beam to teach a clear lesson. He told the people not to fight this discipline, but to accept God's correction with a humble heart. To a proud people, this sounded like an unbearable humiliation.

Think of how a father protects his child. No loving parent lets a child run toward a cliff without grabbing their arm firmly. That quick grab might pinch and leave a red mark, but it keeps the child from falling. God was doing the same thing: giving a firm correction that hurt their pride, but would keep them safe.

In our daily lives, we carry heavy loads too. It might be overdue bills, sickness in the family, or the tiring work of raising children alone. We look for quick escapes from the weight, but running from reality never cures the real problem.

### The sweet poison of easy promises

In that fearful time, a man named Hananiah stepped forward. He claimed to speak for God and told the crowd exactly what they wanted to hear. He promised that within two short years, all problems would end and riches would return without anyone needing to change.

To convince the people, Hananiah pulled the wooden yoke from Jeremiah's neck and broke it on the ground. Everyone cheered in relief. Who does not want to hear that everything will be fine without any hard work or honest regret?

Yet those sweet words were empty lies. Jeremiah returned with a serious message from God: the wood was broken, but an iron yoke would take its place. Hananiah had fooled the people with easy hope, and he met a bitter end for mocking the truth.

Today, many voices still sell easy shortcuts. The internet is full of quick ways to get rich, telling you that you never have to admit your faults to be blessed. These soothing lies are dangerous because they offer a fast escape while leaving our hearts weak and sick inside.

### The Father who holds your hand

The secret to true freedom is not pretending that nothing is wrong. It comes from knowing who walks beside us. Jeremiah cried, felt afraid, and was mocked, but God was never ashamed of him. To God, a humble heart that seeks truth matters far more than the cheers of a crowd.

God loves us like a good father who will not trade our healing for cheap praise. Jesus Christ came to this earth and carried a heavy cross on His own shoulders to free us from selfishness. He did not run from the pain of truth, so that we could find real forgiveness.

You do not need a perfect life to be accepted. When we drop the mask of comfortable lies and ask for help, we find God's open arms ready to welcome us and make us whole again.

### A practical next step

Pick one thing in your life that you have been putting off because you are afraid—an honest talk with your spouse, facing a real debt, or saying sorry to someone you hurt. Today, take one small step toward the truth, and pray: "Lord, give me courage to live in truth, knowing Your love will hold me up."
